FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Booking and who can book

Who can book a consultation?

Women living in Nigeria can book. You need to be in Nigeria during the call. A relative can book and pay on your behalf.

Do I need a referral from my own doctor?

No. You can book directly. If you have reports, scans or letters from another doctor, you can share them securely so the consultant can review them.

How do I book?
  • Choose a service and see the fee.
  • Pick a day and a time (Nigerian time).
  • Give your contact details and consent.
  • Pay, and receive your confirmation with a what-to-bring checklist.

Consultations and second opinions

Is a video consultation a diagnosis?

A video consultation lets the consultant listen to your history, review any results you have and advise you. Some conditions need an examination or tests in person. When that is the case, the consultant will explain what is needed and coordinate it with a local hospital.

What is a second opinion?

A second opinion is an independent review of your existing records and treatment plan by a consultant, followed by a video call and a written summary. You do not have to transfer your care.

What should I bring to my appointment?
  • Any reports, scan results or blood tests
  • A list of medicines you take
  • A short note of your symptoms and when they started
  • Questions you want answered
What if I need tests, a scan or surgery?

The consultant can explain which tests you may need and coordinate them with a partner hospital in Nigeria. Any procedure is carried out by a doctor who is licensed in Nigeria.

Fees and payment

How much does a consultation cost?

Fees are shown in Nigerian naira (₦) on the fees page and on each time slot, before you pay. The current fees are provisional until the consultant confirms them.

Can a relative pay for me?

Yes. A relative or friend can book and pay for a patient in Nigeria. Receipts are issued in the payer's name.

What is the cancellation and refund policy?

The full policy is set out in the booking terms before you pay. Please read it when you book.

Privacy and safety

How is my information kept private?

Your health information is handled under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. Medical records are shared through a secure step, never through the ordinary contact form.

What should I do in an emergency?

This service does not provide emergency care. If you have severe pain, very heavy bleeding, fainting or any emergency, go to your nearest emergency department or call your local emergency number straight away.
